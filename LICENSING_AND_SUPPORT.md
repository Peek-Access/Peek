# Licensing & Support

This page explains, in plain terms, who can use Peek, on what terms, and where to go
for help. It doesn't change the license — [LICENSE](LICENSE) is the only legally
binding document — it just lays out the support model around it, the way
[NV Access does for NVDA](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/?tab=License-1-ov-file).

## Software license

Peek is licensed under the **GNU General Public License v3.0** (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)).

That means anyone — an individual, a company of any size, a government agency, a
school, a non-profit — can use, study, modify, and redistribute Peek free of charge,
under the same terms. The GPL does not allow discriminating between fields of
endeavor, so there is no "free tier" and no "enterprise tier" of the software itself:
the code you get is the same code everyone gets, and it always will be.

What follows is *not* about the code — it's about where Peek's maintainers choose to
spend limited time, and how organizations that want more than the community can
reasonably provide can get it.

## Name and branding

"Peek" and the Peek logo identify this project and its official builds. You're free
to fork, modify, and redistribute the software under the GPL, but please don't use
the Peek name or logo in a way that implies your fork is the official project or is
endorsed by its maintainers — call it something else, or say clearly that it's an
unofficial modified version. This restriction only touches naming/branding; it
doesn't limit any right the GPL grants over the code itself (GPLv3 §7(c)/(e)).

## Community support (everyone, free)

Anyone using Peek — regardless of who they are — can:

- Report bugs and request features via [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/Peek-Access/Peek/issues)
- Ask questions and share configurations via [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/Peek-Access/Peek/discussions)
- Read the [User Guide](docs/USER_GUIDE.md)

This is best-effort, volunteer-driven, and comes with no response-time guarantee —
same as most open-source projects.

## Government & non-profit support

Peek exists to make screens legible to people who need it, and cost shouldn't be
what stands between a government agency, a school, or a non-profit and getting it
set up properly. Within reasonable limits, the maintainers will provide, **at no
charge**, to verified government bodies, public institutions, and registered
non-profit/charitable organizations:

- Help assessing whether Peek fits a specific accessibility need
- Guidance on deployment and configuration (including AI provider and TTS setup)
- Usage training and onboarding support for staff or end users

### Scope and limits

- This is goodwill support, not a contractual service — it's provided as
  maintainer time allows, with no guaranteed response time or outcome.
- It covers guidance and configuration, not bespoke source-code changes or new
  feature development — that falls under commercial services below, and the
  maintainers may, at their discretion, extend the same no-charge terms to a
  request that's clearly small in scope.
- To request it, open a [GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/Peek-Access/Peek/discussions)
  or issue tagged `government-nonprofit-support`, and briefly identify your
  organization and its public-interest mission. Self-declaration is sufficient;
  the maintainers reserve the right to ask clarifying questions for organizations
  they can't otherwise identify.

## Commercial services (paid)

For organizations — typically businesses — that need more than community support
can offer, the maintainers offer paid services on top of the free software:

- **Custom development** — features, integrations, or behavior specific to your
  environment
- **System integration** — deploying and wiring Peek into existing enterprise
  toolchains, MDM/imaging pipelines, or AI infrastructure
- **Technical consulting & training** — accessibility audits, staff training, and
  architecture guidance

These are optional services layered on top of the free, GPL-licensed software —
paying for them changes nothing about your rights to use Peek itself, and not
paying for them changes nothing about your right to use Peek for free.

To discuss a paid engagement, open a
[GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/Peek-Access/Peek/discussions) describing
what you need; the maintainers will follow up on scope, timeline, and cost from
there.
